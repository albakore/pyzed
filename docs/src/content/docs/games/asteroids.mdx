*Rock Storm* is a shooter in the spirit of the 1979 classic: a ship that turns and thrusts, drifts with
inertia, and shoots rocks that split into smaller rocks. It is a good place to learn a bit of trigonometry (it
is only `cos` and `sin`) and to make the screen come alive with **particles**.

## What you'll learn

- Turn an **angle** into a direction with `cos` and `sin`; thrust and **drag**.
- **Wrap** things around the edges of the screen.
- **Circle collisions** for rocks, bullets and the ship.
- **Particles** with `game.Particles`: an engine trail, muzzle flashes, debris and explosions.
- **Screen shake**, invulnerability after a hit and waves.

<Steps>

1. **An angle becomes a direction.** The ship stores where it points in degrees. `cos` and `sin` of that
   angle give a direction of length 1: at 0° it is `(1, 0)` (right), at -90° it is `(0, -1)` (up). Thrust adds
   that direction to the velocity; drag takes away a fraction every second:

   ```python
   def fly(self, dt):
       self.angle += game.axis(("left", "a"), ("right", "d"), held=self.held) * TURN * dt
       rad = math.radians(self.angle)
       fx, fy = math.cos(rad), math.sin(rad)
       if game.is_pressed("up", "w", held=self.held):
           self.vx += fx * THRUST * dt
           self.vy += fy * THRUST * dt
       damping = max(0.0, 1 - DRAG * dt)
       self.vx, self.vy = self.vx * damping, self.vy * damping
   ```

   The ship keeps drifting after you let go: that inertia is what makes Asteroids feel like space.

2. **Wrap around.** What leaves through one edge comes back through the other. The modulo operator `%` does
   it in one line; the margin lets big rocks leave the screen completely before they reappear:

   ```python
   def wrap(x, y, margin=0):
       return ((x + margin) % (W + 2 * margin) - margin, (y + margin) % (H + 2 * margin) - margin)
   ```

3. **Rocks with character.** A rock is a circle for collisions, but drawn as a jagged polygon: points around a
   circle at slightly random distances, turned by its own angle every frame:

   ```python
   n = 9 + size * 2
   self.outline = [(math.cos(i / n * math.tau), math.sin(i / n * math.tau), rnd.uniform(0.75, 1.1))
                   for i in range(n)]
   ```

4. **Circle collisions.** A bullet hits a rock when it is closer to the rock's center than its radius; the
   ship hits a rock when the distance is less than both radii added:

   ```python
   for b in self.bullets[:]:
       for rock in self.rocks:
           if math.hypot(b[0] - rock.x, b[1] - rock.y) < rock.r:
               self.bullets.remove(b)
               self.break_rock(rock)
               break
   ```

   Iterate over a copy (`self.bullets[:]`) when you remove items from the list inside the loop.

5. **Split the rock.** A big rock (size 3) becomes two medium ones, a medium becomes two small, a small one is
   gone. Each piece flies off in a random direction, a little faster than its parent:

   ```python
   def break_rock(self, rock):
       self.rocks.remove(rock)
       self.score += POINTS[rock.size]
       self.sparks.burst(rock.x, rock.y, count=10 + rock.size * 10, color=["#cbd5e1", "#94a3b8", "#fbbf24"],
                         speed=(40, 120 + rock.size * 60), life=(0.3, 0.6 + rock.size * 0.25), size=(1, 3))
       self.shake.kick(rock.size * 2.5)
       if rock.size > 1:
           for _ in range(2):
               self.rocks.append(Rock(self.random, rock.x, rock.y, rock.size - 1,
                                      math.hypot(rock.vx, rock.vy) * 1.3 + self.random.uniform(20, 60)))
   ```

6. **Particles everywhere.** `game.Particles` is a list of short-lived dots. `burst` adds some, `update(dt)`
   moves them and removes the dead ones, `shapes()` draws them fading out. The engine trail is a small burst
   *every frame* while thrusting, aimed backwards:

   ```python
   self.trail = game.Particles(drag=3.0, limit=400, seed=seed)
   ...
   self.trail.burst(self.x - fx * SHIP_R, self.y - fy * SHIP_R, count=3, angle=self.angle + 180, spread=30,
                    speed=(80, 180), life=(0.15, 0.4), size=(1.5, 3), color=["#fbbf24", "#f97316", "#fde68a"])
   ```

   <Media clip="concept-particles" alt="Four particle effects side by side: an orange explosion, a cyan fountain falling back under gravity, pink glowing sparks, and a yellow-green trail following a moving dot" caption="One class, four effects: burst(), a fountain (angle=-90, a narrow spread and gravity), glow=True, and a trail emitted every frame." size="narrow" />

   `angle` and `spread` aim a burst (`angle=-90, spread=40` is a fountain), `gravity` and `drag` shape the
   fall, `life` and `size` are ranges picked at random per particle, and `color` can be a list to mix.

7. **Getting hit.** A crash costs a life, shakes the screen hard and scatters the ship as particles. Then the
   ship comes back in the middle and blinks for two seconds (`safe`), when rocks can't hurt it.

</Steps>

<Aside type="tip" title="Particles are cheap, but not free">
Each particle is a circle (two with `glow=True`). A few hundred alive at once is nothing; tens of thousands is a
lot of Python per frame. Use `limit=` to cap each emitter, and shorter `life` before smaller `count`.
</Aside>
