<Aside type="note" title="In plain words">
The cables of the rack behave like real ones: they hang, they bounce a little when you plug them
in, and small dots run along them in the direction the signal travels.
</Aside>

## The shape: a hanging curve

A cable between two jacks is a **cubic Bézier curve**: it starts at one jack, ends at the other,
and two control points pull it down. The control points sit a little inside the two ends and
lower by the **sag**, which grows with the distance between the jacks:

```
sag = (18 + 0.3 × distance) × (1.15 − tension)
```

so a short cable is almost straight and a long one droops, as gravity would make it. `tension`
(0..1) tightens all of them at once.

```python
from pyzed.ui import rack

c0, c1 = rack.cable_path((40, 60), (300, 80), tension=0.45)
shape = draw.path([("M", 40, 60), ("C", *c0, *c1, 300, 80)], stroke="#f5c542", width=5)
```

## The drop: a damped spring

When a cable is plugged, its sag does not appear at once. It follows a **damped spring**:

```
drop(t) = 1 − e^(−5.5 t) · cos(13 t)
```

At `t = 0` the cable is straight; it falls, goes a little past its resting sag (the cosine makes
it overshoot) and settles in about half a second. The rack stores when each cable was plugged and
multiplies its sag by `drop(now − plugged)` on every frame. Nothing is simulated step by step, so
the motion is the same at any frame rate.

## The look

Each cable is drawn as five layers, from the bottom up:

1. A soft **shadow**, offset down and right, so the cable floats over the panels.
2. A darker **outline**.
3. The **body** in the cable's color (`cable_opacity` lets you see the panels through it).
4. A thin white **shine** along the top edge, which makes it read as round.
5. Two **plugs**: a ring, a lighter center and a highlight.

New cables take the next color of `ui.rack.CABLE_COLORS` (yellow, red, green, blue, purple...),
like VCV Rack. A cable keeps its color when you pick it up and move it.

## Signal flow

With `flow=True` small light dots travel from the output to the input, spaced about 38 px apart.
Their position along the curve is the time multiplied by a speed, wrapped around, so they move
smoothly at any frame rate and every cable shows which way its signal goes.

## While dragging

A cable being dragged follows the pointer with a tighter, lighter curve and **snaps** to the
nearest compatible jack within 18 px (outputs only reach inputs and the other way round), so you
see where it will land before you let go. An input takes one cable: dropping on a patched input
replaces its cable.

<Aside type="tip">
The rack needs to redraw every frame for the drop and the flow: `pz.frames(lambda dt: None)` in
`subscriptions()`. Set `flow=False` and return no subscription when nothing moves to let the app
sleep.
</Aside>
